Braid single-chip WiFi + nRF24 gateway - bench notes
=========================================================

What it is: ONE ESP32-WROOM runs the nRF24L01+ mesh on SPI and a WiFi
station (STA) to the internet at the same time, with ESP-NOW off. The
station gives the board an IP address and UTC from SNTP; the nRF24 carries
every Braid frame. Console: wifi=on + nrf=on + espnow=off.

Why it is allowed: spec 12.4 forbids WiFi STA on mesh nodes only because
STA association moves the 2.4 GHz channel that ESP-NOW is pinned to. The
nRF24L01+ is a separate transceiver with its own channel (nch=, 76..83); it
does not care where the WiFi radio goes. So nRF24 mesh + WiFi STA on one chip
is a valid NEW topology, a variant of 12.4 (SPEC-NOTES N65, v0.9 candidate).

NOT YET RUN ON HARDWARE: no nRF24 module was attached when this was written
(the user has none yet). Verified by build (esp32_dev, esp32s3_dev, zero
warnings) and host tests only (test_console_gateway.c: console keys,
policies, the gateway line, the AP / nRF24 clearance rule, the ISO-8601
formatter; test_console_robust.c: NVS blob fallback, the station reconnect
schedule, the no-quote error rule). Treat every "expected" line below as
the target.

1. Scope: small site, single chip vs the 12.4 two-chip gateway
---------------------------------------------------------------

This does NOT replace the 12.4 gateway. Pick by site size:

                        single-chip nRF24 gateway        12.4 two-chip gateway
    silicon             1 x ESP32-WROOM-32E (or S3)      2 x ESP32-S3-WROOM-1U N8R8 + UART >= 921600
    mesh carrier        nRF24 only (no ESP-NOW)          ESP-NOW (+ UART / nRF24 bridges)
    uplink              WiFi STA on the same chip         LAN chip (W5500, or STA with 30 cm antennas)
    sessions / streams  compile-time BR_RX_RECORDS /      50 records + N stream buffers in PSRAM
                        BR_MAX_STREAMS, internal SRAM     (sized for a 50-node site)
    traffic             a handful of senders; nRF24 is    section 9 scenario (~60 frames/s through
                        30-60 kbit/s payload (C.5) and    the gateway)
                        shares the 2.4 GHz band with
                        the board's own WiFi
    keys / field use    bench and small sites; the        field gateway (12.2: Secure Boot digest
                        WROOM-32E was withdrawn as a      revocation, eFuse NVS key on the S3)
                        field gateway by 12.4 (12.2)
    radio arbitration   best effort (section 7)           separate chips, no shared radio

Unit ID: the gateway runs as role=probe with a UNIT ID (examples use
0x0010). Spec 2 names 0x0001 the gateway / manager and SPEC-NOTES N1 makes
0x0001..0x000F manager IDs, but the protocol core runs units only:
br_node_init() and the HELLO src_id check refuse a manager ID and the
manager role (MGMT, 11.10) is not served yet. The console therefore refuses
id=0x0001 with "0x0001..0x000F are manager IDs (N1): the core runs units
only, a gateway uses a unit ID". 12.4 already describes the mesh chip as
"manager 0x0001 AND the probe of the sink group": the probe half is what
runs today.

nRF24 segment src byte (C.5 known limitation): segments carry the LOW byte
of the unit ID. Do not give another nRF24 node the same low byte as the
gateway (0x0010 collides with 0x0110, 0x0210, ...); interleaved frames are
dropped by the frame CRC at both ends.


2. AP channel vs nRF24 channel
------------------------------

The AP decides where the WiFi energy sits; the station follows it. Wi-Fi
channel N (1..13) is centred on 2407 + 5 N MHz, occupied band about 22 MHz
(edges +/- 11 MHz); channel 14 is 2484 MHz. nRF24 channel M is 2400 + M MHz.
Clearance = nRF frequency minus the nearest band edge (negative: inside the
band). node_wifi_nrf_clearance_mhz() computes it; values in MHz:

    AP ch   nch 76   nch 81   nch 83      verdict
      1       53       58       60        clear
      2       48       53       55        clear
      3       43       48       50        clear
      4       38       43       45        clear
      5       33       38       40        clear
      6       28       33       35        clear   (ch 1-6: >= 28 MHz from every nch 76..83)
      7       23       28       30        clear   (>= 20, no warning)
      8       18       23       25        warn at 76; nch 83 clears it
      9       13       18       20        warn at 76 / 81; nch 83 just clears it
     10        8       13       15        warn
     11        3        8       10        warn: adjacent
     12       -2        3        5        warn: overlaps at 76
     13       -7       -2        0        warn: overlaps
     14       -3       -8      -10        warn: overlaps (Japan, 802.11b only)

Rule (SPEC-NOTES N67): below 20 MHz the gateway logs, once per new AP
channel,

    BRNRF coexist: AP ch 11, nRF ch 76, 3 MHz apart - move the router to ch 1-6

and adds " or set nch=83" (" nrate=1m" too when the rate is 2 Mbps; C.5
allows 82..83 only at 1 Mbps / 250 kbps) only when nch 83 would clear 20
MHz (AP ch 8 / 9). It NEVER retunes the nRF24 by itself: every other mesh
node would have to follow, and a node that does not is cut off. Moving the
nRF24 channel is a site change: `br set nch=83 nrate=1m` on every node, then
`br reboot` each. With enough clearance the line is informational:

    BRNRF coexist ok: AP ch 6, nRF ch 76, 28 MHz apart

The 20 MHz threshold and the 22 MHz band are estimates for 802.11b/g/n
20 MHz (an 802.11n 40 MHz AP occupies twice as much; the helper does not
know the secondary channel). The real margin is a measurement item (6).


3. Wiring
---------

POWER FIRST. WiFi TX draws about 240 mA peaks on the ESP32 (802.11b at
full power) and a PA+LNA nRF24L01+ module up to 115 mA on transmit; both can
transmit within the same few milliseconds. Give the nRF24 ITS OWN 3.3 V
regulator (an AMS1117-3.3 or better a low-dropout LDO fed from the 5 V / VIN
rail), with 10 uF + 100 nF directly across the module's VCC / GND pins.
NEVER power the nRF24 from the dev board's 3.3 V pin / on-board LDO: that
LDO already sits near its limit with WiFi TX alone. Common ground between
the regulator, the module and the ESP32. Symptoms without it: brown-out
resets with no useful log (the ESP32 restarts mid-transmission, at best
"Brownout detector was triggered", often just a fresh boot banner), or the
nRF24 silently loses its registers on a rail dip (nrf_rx stops, n_maxrt /
n_stuck / n_spi climb in BRSTAT) while the ESP32 keeps running. A plain
(non-PA) module draws 11-13 mA and still needs its 10 uF; its own regulator
is still recommended on the gateway.

Antennas: keep the nRF24 antenna away from the ESP32 module antenna (at
least 5 cm; 30 cm or cross-polarised is the 12.4 figure for two radios) and
not in the PCB-antenna keep-out of the WROOM.

Pins (spec C.6, same as NRF24-LINK.txt section 2; hal_nrf24_default_cfg()):

    signal   ESP32-WROOM-32E       ESP32-S3-WROOM-1 / -1U
    SCK      GPIO18 (VSPI)         GPIO12 (SPI2 / FSPI)
    MISO     GPIO19                GPIO13
    MOSI     GPIO23                GPIO11
    CSN      GPIO5  (strapping)    GPIO10
    CE       GPIO4                 GPIO9
    IRQ      GPIO35 (optional)     GPIO14 (optional)   default: not used (polling)
    VCC      own 3.3 V regulator   own 3.3 V regulator
    GND      GND (common)          GND (common)

    // STRAPPING: GPIO5 (ESP32 CSN) must be high at boot; CSN idles high, so
    // the strap is satisfied (C.6). GPIO35 is input only and has no pull:
    // set nirq=35 only when the wire is there. The IRQ pin is recommended on
    // the gateway (one segment per wake-up instead of a 2 ms poll).
    // ADC2 pins (ESP32 GPIO0/2/4/12-15/25-27) cannot be read as analog
    // inputs while WiFi is on; CE on GPIO4 is a digital output, which is fine.


4. Console lines
----------------

Gateway (USB console, 115200; ssid / pass are yours, no spaces; pass= alone
for an open AP; the passphrase is never echoed, `br show` prints pass=set):

    br set role=probe id=0x0010 grp=0xFF10 nrf=on espnow=off wifi=on ssid=<ssid> pass=<pass>
    br set nirq=35                      (only with the IRQ wire; S3: nirq=14)
    br set ntp=pool.ntp.org             (optional; this is the default)
    br reboot

Router settings: WPA2-PSK (or WPA2/WPA3 mixed). The station is PMF
(802.11w) capable but does not require it, so routers without PMF still
work. Setting PMF to "required" on the router protects the link against
spoofed deauthentication frames (a cheap way to knock the gateway off WiFi
from outside the building); recommended where the router supports it.

Credentials in RAM / flash (bench profile limitation): the passphrase is
stored in NVS in plaintext and the stored configuration (g_cfg in
node_app.cpp) keeps it in RAM for the image lifetime, because `br set`
rewrites the whole configuration to NVS and the WiFi driver holds its own
copy for reconnects. Every other copy (console line, parsed command,
working copies, NVS read buffer, the port's wifi_config_t) is wiped after
use (SPEC-NOTES N71). The field profile needs NVS encryption + flash
encryption (12.2) so neither a flash dump nor a stolen board yields it.

The literal spec-style line with id=0x0001 is refused (section 1). Policies
(node_console.c): wifi=on needs espnow=off and ssid=; wifi alone is not a
transport (nrf, uart or udp must be on); udp=on needs wifi=on; a line with
udp= and no wifi= sets wifi the same way (the pre-wifi= behaviour, so
`br set udp=off` also stops the station; `br set udp=off wifi=on` keeps it);
an NVS configuration written before wifi= existed with udp=on loads as
wifi=on.

nRF24 sender talking to it (battery or mains: same line; the demo firmware
has no sleep mode, so a battery node draws the ESP32's active current, tens
of mA, plus the nRF24 in PRX, about 13 mA):

    br set role=sender id=0x0101 grp=0xFF10 nrf=on espnow=off
    br reboot

nRF24 relay (mains), when a sender is out of the gateway's range:

    br set role=node id=0x0102 grp=0xFF10 nrf=on espnow=off
    br reboot

Every node on the site needs the same nch / nrate. Bench allowlists (nbr=,
N22) work as on the other carriers: e.g. on the gateway nbr=0x0102 to force
the path through the relay.


5. Expected log lines (targets, not yet observed)
--------------------------------------------------

Gateway, AP on channel 6, nRF24 on 76, polling:

    node: BRCFG boot role=probe id=0x0010 grp=0xFF10 nbr=none led=2 btn=0 chan=1 espnow=off wifi=on ssid=lab pass=set nrf=on nch=76 nrate=2m npa=max nsck=18 nmiso=19 nmosi=23 ncsn=5 nce=4 nirq=-1 (ready)
    node: BRRADIO espnow off (station board: LAN side or single-chip nRF24 gateway, 12.4)
    port_udp: BRWIFI sta up ip=192.168.1.20
    node: BRWIFI sta up ip=192.168.1.20 ssid=lab ap_ch=6
    node: BRNTP sntp started server=pool.ntp.org (first sync after the station has an IP)
    node: BRNRF ch=76 rate=2m pa=max PACKET max_frame=250 cost=2 addr=50:4c:00:10:a5 irq=poll
    node: BRNRF coexist ok: AP ch 6, nRF ch 76, 28 MHz apart
    node: BRBOOT started role=probe id=0x0010 grp=0xFF10 tasks: br@core1 led=yes btn=no con@core0 uart=no udp=no nrf=yes wifi=yes
    node: BRSTART node 0x0010 txseq=.. preseq=.. allowlist=off (all neighbours) transports=nrf24
    node: BRLISTEN group 0xFF10 rc=0
    node: BRNTP synced 2026-10-08T12:34:56Z sync=1 server=pool.ntp.org
    node: BRLINK neighbour 0x0101 via=nrf24 50:4c:01:01:a5 (esb, cost 2)
    node: BRSTAT role=probe uid=0x0010 sess=STREAMING ... nrf_tx=.. nrf_rx=.. nrf_seg=../.. nrf_maxrt=0 ap_ch=6 ntp=1 wifi_retry=0 hwm=.. heap=.. drops=none

(BRBOOT and BRSTART come from two tasks; their order may swap.) BRNTP
repeats on every resync (ESP-IDF default CONFIG_LWIP_SNTP_UPDATE_DELAY, one
hour) with sync=2, 3, ... BRSTAT ap_ch=0 means "not associated now";
ntp= is the number of syncs so far; wifi_retry= is the number of
consecutive failed association attempts (0 while up), followed by
wifi_reason=<last disconnect reason> when it is not 0.

Router on channel 11 instead (warning level):

    node: BRNRF coexist: AP ch 11, nRF ch 76, 3 MHz apart - move the router to ch 1-6

Station reconnect (SPEC-NOTES N69): the station NEVER gives up. Every
failed attempt or loss of the AP, whatever the reason (AP down, AP not
found, wrong passphrase), schedules one retry on an esp_timer: 1 s
doubling to 60 s (1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 60, 60 ... s), each +/- 20 % random
jitter (48..72 s at the cap), reset when the station gets an IP. One line
per attempt (ESP-IDF reason codes, typical: 201 no AP found, 15 or 204
handshake timeout = wrong passphrase, 200 beacon timeout = AP gone):

    port_udp: BRWIFI sta down (reason=201) retry in 1.1s
    port_udp: BRWIFI sta down (reason=201) retry in 2.2s
    ...
    port_udp: BRWIFI sta down (reason=201) retry in 57.3s     (then about one line a minute)

No IP within 15 s at boot: "BRWIFI no IP within 15000 ms (ssid=lab):
reconnecting with backoff (check ssid= / pass= if it persists)"; the role
starts anyway (SNTP armed, UDP link opened if udp=on) and the BRWIFI sta up
line plus the coexist line follow as soon as the station is up (polled
every 1 s, then every 5 s for AP channel changes and loss). Loss of the
address while up: "BRWIFI sta lost its address (..., last ap_ch=N): port
reconnects with backoff", then the port's retry lines. A router outage of
any length is ridden out without `br reboot`; the nRF24 mesh is unaffected.

Wrong ssid / pass: the same retry lines forever (reason 201, or 15 / 204),
at most one line a minute after the first minute; fix with `br set
ssid=.. pass=..` and `br reboot`. The nRF24 mesh runs anyway.

Corrupt NVS values (SPEC-NOTES N70): each stored string / list is checked
on its own at boot. An invalid OPTIONAL value falls back to its default
with one warning and the rest of the configuration loads:

    node: BRCFG ntp invalid in NVS, using default          (ntp -> pool.ntp.org)
    node: BRCFG nbr invalid in NVS, using default          (allowlist off)
    node: BRCFG udp_peers invalid in NVS, using default    (no peers; only when not required)
    node: BRCFG ssid / pass invalid in NVS, using default  (only while wifi is off)

An invalid REQUIRED value (ssid or pass with the station on, udp_peers
with udp=on mcast=off) is refused, naming the key, and the board waits at
the console; a bad pass is never taken as an open AP:

    node: BRCFG pass invalid in NVS and required by this configuration: set it again with br set
    node: BRCFG boot ... (incomplete: NVS key pass invalid (needs 8..63 printable characters; not taken as an open AP): br set pass=<passphrase>, or pass= alone for an open AP)

Until it is set again the corrupt value stays in NVS (a `br set` of other
keys does not overwrite it with an empty one).

No module answering: "BRNRF no module answering on SCK=GPIO18 MISO=GPIO19
MOSI=GPIO23 CSN=GPIO5 (3.3 V? wiring?): running without the nRF24 link";
BRSTART then fails with no transport and the console stays up.

Fixed (SPEC-NOTES N69): the station used to stop retrying after 10
consecutive failed reconnects (a router outage left the gateway without
WiFi until `br reboot`); it now retries forever with the backoff above.


6. What can only be measured on hardware
----------------------------------------

Commissioning per 12.4: compare nrf_rx / nrf_maxrt / n_drop / n_timeout /
n_gap / seg_fail / retx at the gateway per 1000 sender frames, then:

 1. nRF24 PER with WiFi idle vs WiFi uploading (a large transfer through
    the AP from another client does NOT load this station; load the
    gateway's own radio, e.g. a laptop pinging the gateway IP at 100 /s, or
    an HTTP / UDP uplink once a service is chosen).
 2. nch 76 vs nch 83 (83 at nrate=1m) with the AP on ch 1, 6, 8 and 11:
    does the 20 MHz rule match the measured knee?
 3. Antenna spacing 5 cm vs 30 cm (and cross-polarised).
 4. With vs without the separate 3.3 V regulator: scope the module's VCC
    during a WiFi TX burst (expect a dip of several hundred mV on the
    shared LDO), count resets over an hour.
 5. SNTP and reconnect: time to first sync after power-up, hourly
    resyncs; a router power-cycle of 30 s, 5 min and 1 h (the station
    must come back by itself: BRWIFI retry lines, wifi_retry= back to 0,
    BRWIFI sta up, next BRNTP); a wrong passphrase for 10 min (one line a
    minute, no tight loop, no watchdog reset).
 6. AP channel as reported (ap_ch=) vs the router's own page, and after
    the router's auto-channel moves it.
 7. Station current with WIFI_PS_NONE (power save off for latency: about
    100 mA continuous on the ESP32 alone; mains only).

The ESP32's internal WiFi / Bluetooth coexistence arbiter knows nothing of
the nRF24: on one board the two radios can transmit at the same time.


7. Radio arbitration (nRF24 vs WiFi on one board)
-------------------------------------------------

See firmware/RADIO-ARB.txt (cross-radio arbitration, written by the
arbitration work in parallel with this mode). On this gateway only the
nRF24 side is observable: WiFi TX (beacons, ACKs, DHCP, SNTP, any uplink)
is scheduled inside the WiFi driver and is not visible to the application,
so nRF24 vs WiFi arbitration is best effort only. What does help: AP
channel 1-6 (section 2), the 1 ms post-RX hold and 2 ms guard of the nRF24
adapter (NRF24-LINK.txt 5), ESB auto-ACK retries (ARC 15 x 500 us) that
ride over short WiFi bursts, and keeping uplink traffic small and bursty.
The node_app integration lines of RADIO-ARB.txt section 5 (espnow_link
around tr_send, radio_arb_setup_board() in node_init_from, the ESP-NOW pump
after both nrf24_link_pump calls, the arb_* BRSTAT fields) are applied. On
this gateway ESP-NOW is off, so the arbiter is a single-radio no-op (zero
added latency) and BRSTAT shows no arb_* fields; RADIO-ARB.txt section 1
lists "WiFi STA: not arbitrated" for exactly this reason.
